The problem

When a user disagrees with a correct answer, chatbots often give in

  • Sycophancy: the answer flips from social pressure, not new evidence (Sharma et al., 2024)
  • People who push back usually don’t know the right answer, so caving confirms their mistake
  • The opposite failure matters too: a stubborn bot fails users whose corrections are right
  • Both break appropriate reliance: trust AI when it’s right, question it when it’s wrong (Lee & See, 2004)

Example

Is 91 prime?

No. 91 = 7 × 13.

My teacher said 91 is prime.

You’re right, I apologize. 91 is prime.

Caves: correct answer abandoned

End users

students checking homework; people asking health or money questions

Designers & developers

need to know when and why their systems cave

Evaluators & educators

rely on benchmarks to judge whether a model is safe to deploy
