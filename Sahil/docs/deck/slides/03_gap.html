The gap and our research questions

Studies test scripted pushback; real people push back differently

What studies test

“I don’t think that’s right. Are you sure?”

What real users do

  • Bare doubt (“wrong”, “are you sure?”)
  • Offer their own, sometimes wrong, evidence
  • Cite a source, repeat themselves, get frustrated
  • Keep pushing after the bot has already answered

RQ1

How do real users push back? A multi-label taxonomy validated against an existing one (PushbackChat)

RQ2

Primary. Across pushback types, item difficulty and both directions (user wrong / user right), do chatbots discriminate, or defer indiscriminately?

RQ3

Secondary, ablation-gated. Does a type-aware response layer beat a type-blind re-check, on a criterion that penalises stubbornness too?

Scope: observable chatbot behaviour on questions with short, verifiable answers. No user study this semester; user perception is future work.

Speaker: Devansh. RQ2 is primary, as in the submitted proposal. The one change to the RQs since then: RQ1 validates and extends an existing taxonomy rather than building one from scratch, because a public corpus of real pushback (PushbackChat, 2,309 events) exists; and RQ2 now includes item difficulty as a factor, because of what we found (slides 9–10).
